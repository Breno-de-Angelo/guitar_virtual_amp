\section{Products and Open-Source Tools}
\label{sec:products}

Products use three kinds of input (Table~\ref{tab:products}).

\emph{Capture of physical equipment.} Kemper profiling plays test signals into
the amplifier and analyses its response; compressors and time-based effects
must be bypassed~\cite{kemper2018manual}. Neural DSP's Neural Capture,
IK Multimedia's TONEX (with parametric models announced in October 2026),
Line 6's Proxy and NAM work the same way with neural
models~\cite{neuraldsp2026qcmanual,ik2026parametric,line6ndproxy,atkinsonndnam}.
None accepts a recording. The Quad Cortex manual does say that a capture's gain
metadata is estimated from the device's dynamic range and harmonic content,
the only vendor statement we found of a gain descriptor computed from
audio~\cite{neuraldsp2026qcmanual}.

\emph{Spectral matching to a recording.} The Axe-Fx III Tone Match block
computes long-term averaged spectra of a reference and of the user's preset
and applies their smoothed difference as a filter, which can be exported as a
cabinet impulse response~\cite{fractalndtonematch}. Its manual lists the
conditions: a starting preset with the right amplifier and the right amount of
distortion, no effects, an isolated and unclipped guitar track, matched
loudness, mono analysis, and broad playing material. iZotope's Match EQ does
the same for mixes~\cite{izotope2019matcheq}. These tools estimate only the
linear part, after the user has fixed the nonlinear one.

\emph{Presets from audio, song names or text.} Positive Grid's BIAS X
``Music-to-Tone'' accepts a file of up to 15 minutes, full mixes included,
finds a guitar segment and returns a full chain; the feature runs online and
no method or accuracy is published~\cite{positivegridndbiasxhelp,positivegrid2025biasxai}.
Positive Grid co-authored the tone-embedding amplifier model of Chen et
al.~\cite{chen2024zeroshot}, but does not say whether BIAS X uses it. Small
third-party apps generate presets from a song name with a language model's
knowledge of the artist's equipment, sometimes with stem separation and coarse
spectral features~\cite{bouharb2026gptonebuilder,aitonetechndgenerator}; none
publishes an evaluation. The most detailed public design is a Chinese patent
granted in 2025: separation, a tone-type classifier, a network that picks an
amplifier and cabinet model and regresses five knobs, a fit of up to 24
peaking filters to the difference of the spectra, tempo and key analysis for
delay and modulation times, and retrieval from a preset database for the
remaining effects~\cite{huanyin2025presetpatent}.

\emph{Open-source tools.} NAM-EQ-Match renders the player's DI through every
capture in a NAM library at several input gains, compares equalisation-invariant
dynamics features (crest factor, compression, spectral flux, MFCC statistics)
with the target, then fits a matching impulse response or tone-stack
equaliser~\cite{sd4562026nameqmatch}. \texttt{tonematch} predicts seven
amplifier knobs with a mel-spectrogram CNN and refines them by CMA-ES through
a grey-box amplifier, reporting a median log-spectral error of 1.54\,dB on 24
isolated Guitar-TECHS cases, against 3.68\,dB with every knob at
noon~\cite{neuwirth2026tonematch}.
ToneRunner retrieves plugin chains with a contrastive encoder and documents
how the encoder learns the riff instead of the tone when the same dry clip is
not shared across chains in training~\cite{nathanben2026tonerunner}.
These projects are small and not peer reviewed, but they converge on the same
design as the research: retrieve or predict a nonlinear core, refine by
rendering, finish with a linear match.
